2. Industry and the triple test

IR Code s.2(p): "industry" IN FORCE

  • It is any systematic activity carried on by co-operation between an employer and a worker (employed directly or through any agency, including a contractor) for the production, supply or distribution of goods or services to satisfy human wants or wishes, not being wants merely spiritual or religious.
  • It is an industry whether or not capital has been invested, and whether or not the activity is carried on for gain or profit.
  • Four exclusions: charitable, social or philanthropic institutions (owned or managed by organisations wholly or substantially engaged in such service); sovereign functions of the appropriate Government; domestic service; any other activity the Central Government notifies.
  • The sovereign-functions exclusion includes all activities of Central Government departments dealing with defence research, atomic energy and space. For the notified exclusion, the saved sources list no notified activity, so its working is open.

Bridge. The Code text follows the 1982 text of ID Act s.2(j), which, the Court said, was never notified. ID Act s.2(j) is repealed. REPEALED The Court said that 1982 amendment was "never notified" (State of U.P. v. Jai Bir Singh, 2026 INSC 897). This note did not check the Gazette. The judgment copy read is a Tier 2 copy.

Exam answer: the triple test (Rajappa, 1978)

Rajappa: three limbs, and what else it held (EPFO-2025 Q6)

In Bangalore Water Supply and Sewerage Board v. A. Rajappa the Supreme Court said an enterprise is prima facie an "industry" where there is: (i) systematic activity, (ii) organised by co-operation between employer and employee, (iii) for the production or distribution of goods and services to satisfy human wants and wishes, not spiritual or religious.

Absence of a profit motive or gainful objective is irrelevant, whether the venture is public, joint, private or other.

Professions, clubs, educational institutions, co-operatives, research institutes and charitable projects cannot be exempted if they meet the triple tests. Small exceptions exist for simple ventures with no real employees and for eleemosynary missions.

Only sovereign functions "strictly understood" are exempt. Welfare activities of economic ventures run by Government are not (the dominant nature test). Chandrachud J, in a separate opinion, would not except sovereign functions at all.

Hospitals: the Court approved State of Bombay v. Hospital Mazdoor Sabha (1960). It overruled other decisions such as Cricket Club of India and Madras Gymkhana.

Facts of the case: decided on 21 February 1978 by a seven-judge Bench; citation 1978 AIR 548 = (1978) 2 SCC 213. The triple-test opinion is the lead opinion of Krishna Iyer J, for himself, Bhagwati J and Desai J. The other judges wrote separately. This note gives no vote split, because sources disagree on it.

PYQ EPFO-2025 Q6 asked which case laid down the triple test for "industry" under the ID Act. Key (b): Bangalore Water Supply and Sewerage Board v. Rajappa (AIR 1978 SC 548). Trap: the publisher's explanation says Rajappa overruled Hospital Mazdoor Sabha. Students repeat it because the explanation sits next to the key. The judgment says the opposite: it approved that case.

Under the Code: the answer is unchanged. The three elements now sit in the text of s.2(p), and the case name still marks their source. IN FORCE But the CJI opinion (para 287(vi)), one of five separate opinions in the 2026 nine-judge bench, said Rajappa "shall not be regarded as the sheet anchor for the interpretation of the IR Code", so s.2(p) is read on its own text. Q6's stem says "under the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947", which is the old Act.

Bridge line: the 2026 nine-judge bench. NOT A CODE RULE It is a court statement on the old Act.

  • State of U.P. v. Jai Bir Singh (2026 INSC 897), decided on 20 August 2026 by a nine-judge bench, reconsidered the triple test for "industry" under ID Act s.2(j). The copy read is a Tier 2 copy. The bench wrote five separate opinions. This note gives no vote count.
  • CJI Surya Kant (for himself, Sharma, Aradhe and Pancholi JJ) said the triple test was "susceptible to further refinement" and "would have been more appropriately structured, hypothetically speaking" in reformulated terms (para 287(ii)). The reformulation is "no more than our considered opinion" and "not intended to displace the governing legal position in respect of pending proceedings" (para 287(iii)). The reformulated test is hypothetical.
  • Matters pending under the ID Act "may be adjudicated in accordance with the Triple Test laid down in Bangalore Water Supply" (para 287(iii)).
  • The CJI opinion refrains from examining the IR Code definition (para 287(v)). Rajappa "shall not be regarded as the sheet anchor for the interpretation of the IR Code" (para 287(vi)). The ruling is confined to disputes under the ID Act (para 288). So IR Code s.2(p) is read on its own text.
Also know: one more opinion in the 2026 case

Datta J (with Bhuyan J), para 76: "Bangalore Water Supply (supra) does not require reconsideration". This is one of five opinions.

4. Strike, lock-out and other forms of conflict

Definitions: Code first IN FORCE

Term IR Code ID Act REPEALED
Strike s.2(zk): a cessation of work by a body of persons employed in any industry acting in combination. It also covers a concerted refusal, or a refusal under a common understanding, of any number of persons who are or have been so employed to continue to work or to accept employment. PYQ EPFO-2020 Q42 asked to match four terms. Key (a): A-1, B-2, C-4, D-3. That is: Closure is the permanent closing down of a place of employment; Workmen are persons employed in any industry to do skilled, unskilled or manual work; Strike is cessation of work by employees; Lockout is the temporary closing down of the workplace by management. Right to strike (APFC-2023 Q39): exam convention

  • Key (c): "The right of workers to strike is a common law right."
  • Sources differ. In T.K. Rangarajan v Government of Tamil Nadu (Supreme Court, 2003) the Court said there is no fundamental, legal or equitable right to go on strike. That case was about government employees.
  • The Code does not grant a right to strike. It regulates strikes (s.62) and makes some illegal (s.63). IN FORCE
  • Learn the key for this exam, and know that sources differ.

Textbook classification of strikes (retrieval cards)

The Code defines only "strike". IN FORCE The list of strike types is a textbook classification, not statute. This note teaches only the types checked against sources.

General, sympathetic, work-to-rule, hunger, token: what do the sources say?

Lecture notes and a journal overview name these types. They are labels, not statute.

Sit-down, stay-in, tool-down, pen-down

Textbook description for the group: workers stay at the workplace without doing their work. In a pen-down strike, clerical workers refuse to use their pens. Sources differ on which name equals which, so do not equate the names. Punjab National Bank v Its Workmen (Supreme Court, 1959) describes a pen-down strike.

Statute link: staying on the premises after working hours (squatting) is an unfair labour practice by workers, Second Schedule Part II item 5. IN FORCE

Lightning strike

A strike without notice (Sadul Textile Mills v Workmen, 1958). Under the Code a strike without the s.62 notice is illegal in an industrial establishment (ss.62, 63(1)). IN FORCE

Other signs of unrest (textbook label)

An IR textbook lists absenteeism and labour turnover as forms of unorganised industrial unrest. The statute uses neither word.

Go-slow, gherao, damage to property: what the Code says IN FORCE

  • Go-slow (Second Schedule Part II item 5, Explanation 1): an occasion when more than one worker in an establishment conjointly work more slowly and with less effort than usual, to persuade the employer to agree to higher pay, better service conditions or another demand (paraphrase of the statute).
  • Staging, encouraging or instigating wilful go-slow, squatting on the work premises after working hours, or "gherao" of any member of the managerial or other staff is an unfair labour practice by workers and Trade Unions (s.84). The statute does not say "illegal", "criminal" or "misconduct".
  • Inciting or indulging in wilful damage to the employer's property connected with the industry is also an unfair labour practice by workers (item 7). The Code never uses the word "sabotage", so that link is only a study aid.
  • Bridge: the ID Act Fifth Schedule Part II item 5 listed go-slow and gherao. The Code carries the item over and adds a definition of go-slow. REPEALED

Case law, kept separate from the statute:

  • Go-slow is serious misconduct (Bank of India v T.S. Kelawala, Supreme Court 1990; Bharat Sugar Mills v Jai Singh, Supreme Court 1961). The Code defines go-slow separately from "strike", but the statute does not say "not a strike". IN FORCE
  • Gherao: the Calcutta High Court (Jay Engineering Works v State of West Bengal, 1967) said a gherao "may or may not amount to a strike, but when accompanied by violence or the commission of any offence, can never be lawful". This is a High Court view, not a Supreme Court rule. No criminal-law section is cited here.

5. The machinery for settling disputes

Names: "preventive" is a label used in commentary for the two bi-partite forums, not a statutory term. The Code heading is "Bi-partite forums" (Chapter II). IN FORCE The note uses neutral headings for the later machinery.

5.1 Route of a dispute under the Code IN FORCE

An individual grievance starts at step 1 and reaches the conciliation officer at step 3 (s.4(8)). At step 4 the officer holds proceedings where a dispute exists or is apprehended, or a s.62 strike notice has been given (s.53(1)).

  1. 1 Worker to Grievance Redressal Committee (GRC), s.4(5). The aggrieved worker files within 1 year from the date the cause of action arises. Rests on rules (manner of filing).
  2. 2 GRC decides, s.4(6), (7). It may finish within 30 days of receiving the application. It decides by majority view, but only if more than half of the worker members agree.
  3. 3 Worker to conciliation officer, s.4(8). Within 60 days of the decision (or of the s.4(6) period ending with no resolution), the worker applies for conciliation through the Trade Union of which he is a member. Rests on rules (manner).
  4. 4 Conciliation officer, s.53. The report is due within 45 days of the start of proceedings, or within 14 days where a s.62 strike notice was received. The parties may extend the time by written agreement, with the officer's approval. The officer cannot hold proceedings after 2 years from the date the dispute arose.
  5. 5 Outcome of conciliation. Settlement: the officer sends a report and the memorandum of settlement signed by the parties to the appropriate Government (s.53(3)). No settlement: the officer sends the parties and the Government a full report, in electronic or other prescribed form, saying why it failed (s.53(4)).
  6. 6 Tribunal. A concerned party applies in the prescribed form within 90 days of receiving the failure report (s.53(6)). An individual worker may instead apply directly after 45 days from the date of the conciliation application (s.4(10)), and before 2 years from the termination (s.4(11)). Rests on rules (prescribed form and manner) and on a Tribunal being in place (s.44(1) notification).
  7. 7 Award. It is communicated to the parties and the appropriate Government (s.55(2)) and becomes enforceable on the expiry of 30 days from communication (s.55(3)).

Two other lanes: employer and workers may refer a dispute to voluntary arbitration by written agreement (s.42(1)). The Central Government may refer a dispute of national importance, or affecting establishments in more than one State, to a National Industrial Tribunal (s.54(1)).

Members and benches

  • NIT members (s.46(3), (4)): the Judicial Member is or has been a High Court Judge. The Administrative Member is or has been a Secretary to the Government of India or of equivalent rank, with adequate experience of labour matters.
  • A Tribunal bench is a Judicial Member with an Administrative Member, a single Judicial Member, or a single Administrative Member (s.44(3)).
  • A two-member bench hears only five types of case: application and interpretation of standing orders; discharge or dismissal of workmen including reinstatement or relief; illegality of a strike or lock-out; retrenchment and closure; Trade Union disputes. All other cases go to a bench of one Judicial Member or one Administrative Member (s.44(7)).

Decisions and limits

  • The Tribunal or NIT decides by consensus of its members. If members differ, they state the points and refer them to the appropriate Government, which appoints a Judicial Member of another Tribunal or NIT to hear them, and the majority view prevails (s.47).
  • No one can be a member of a Tribunal or NIT who is not an independent person or who has reached 65 (s.48).
  • The Code has no schedule listing Labour Court or Tribunal subject matter. Its three Schedules are standing-orders matters (First), unfair labour practices (Second) and conditions of service needing notice of change (Third). The nearest list is s.44(7).
Also know: Administrative Member bar, s.44(4) proviso

A person who has held a post below the rank of Joint Secretary (or equivalent) in the Central or a State Government cannot be appointed Administrative Member of a Tribunal constituted by the Central Government.

  • s.84 (Chapter XII): no employer, worker or Trade Union, whether registered under the Code or not, shall commit any unfair labour practice specified in the Second Schedule. s.2(zo): an unfair labour practice means any of the practices specified in the Second Schedule.
  • The Second Schedule has two parts. Part I is on the part of employers and trade unions of employers (16 items). Part II is on the part of workers and trade unions of workers (items 1 to 8). So the practices bind both sides.
  • Bridge: the ID Act defined it by the Fifth Schedule (s.2(ra)), prohibited it in s.25T, and punished it in s.25U with imprisonment up to 6 months, or a fine up to Rs 1,000, or both. REPEALED The Code uses the Second Schedule and s.86(5), (6).
