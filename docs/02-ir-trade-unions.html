02 Trade Unions

IN FORCE The Industrial Relations Code 2020 (IR Code) is in force from 21 November 2025 (S.O. 5320(E)). Its Chapter III (ss.5 to 27) is the law on trade unions.

REPEALED The Trade Unions Act 1926 stands repealed from the same date: IR Code s.104(1), as substituted by the IR Code (Amendment) Act 2026 (Act 1 of 2026, assent 16 February 2026), which is deemed in force from 21 November 2025 (s.1(2)). The date is derived: the Amendment Act is deemed in force from 21 November 2025 and the repeal runs from that date. S.O. 5320(E) alone did not repeal the Acts (S.O. 5683(E), 8 December 2025, as quoted from the Gazette; the notification text is not in the saved sources); the Amendment Act supplies the repeal.

Old Act sections below are a bridge for old-era PYQs only. Matters left to "such manner as may be prescribed" (such as the verification method) sit in rules and are not covered here.

Snapshot (keep these five)

  1. To register a union under the Code, 7 or more members apply (s.6(1)), and at least 10% of the workers or 100 workers, whichever is less, must be members (s.6(2)). IN FORCE
  2. Registration is optional: the Code says members "may apply" (s.6(1)). "Optional" is a reading of "may apply"; the statute never uses the word. IN FORCE
  3. If more than one registered union works in an establishment, the union with 51% or more of the workers on the muster roll is the sole negotiating union (s.14(3)). IN FORCE Otherwise a negotiating council is formed from unions with at least 20% support (s.14(4)). IN FORCE
  4. A registered union is a body corporate (s.12). Membership age is 14 in a non-hazardous industry (s.20). IN FORCE Office-bearers must be 18 or older (s.21(1)(i)).
  5. Three exam keys to remember. Sources differ on each; learn the key for this exam. Evolutionary unionism is not one of the five Hoxie types (EPFO-2017 Q49). Open shop is "not a union security measure" (EPFO-2017 Q55). The right to strike is a "common law right" (APFC-2023 Q39).

1. What is a trade union

Trade Union, IR Code s.2(zl) IN FORCE

  • It is any combination, temporary or permanent, formed primarily to regulate relations between workers and employers, workers and workers, or employers and employers.
  • It also covers a combination formed to impose restrictive conditions on the conduct of any trade or business.
  • It includes any federation of two or more Trade Unions.

Bridge. The 1926 Act s.2(h) had the same definition. Only the wording changes. The Act says "workmen" (twice), and the Code says "workers".

Webb (History of Trade Unionism, 1920 edition): a trade union is "a continuous association of wage earners for the purpose of maintaining or improving the conditions of their working lives".

Legal person (s.12). IN FORCE Every registered union is a body corporate. It has perpetual succession and a common seal, may hold property and contract, and may sue and be sued. The 1926 Act s.13 said "a corporate" for the same idea.

3. Funds, protections and members

Funds

  • IN FORCE General fund, Code s.15(1): spent only on objects that are prescribed. Bridge: the 1926 Act s.15 listed the heads itself, such as office-bearers' salaries, administration and legal proceedings. It also listed the conduct of trade disputes, compensation of members for loss arising out of trade disputes, and educational, social or religious benefits for members.
  • IN FORCE Separate political fund, Code s.15(2): a registered union may keep one for the civic and political interests of members. No member can be compelled to pay into it, and a member who does not pay is not put at a disadvantage (s.15(3)). Bridge: the 1926 Act put this in s.16.

Trade dispute (old-era PYQ). IN FORCE for the Code counterpart (s.2(q)); the 1926 Act definition is repealed. The 1926 Act s.2(g) defined it as a dispute between employers and workmen, workmen and workmen, or employers and employers, connected with employment or non-employment, the terms of employment or the conditions of labour of any person. The dispute must be connected with employment or non-employment, the terms of employment or the conditions of labour. EPFO-2020 Q46 key (c). The words "trade dispute" do not appear in the Code. Its counterpart is "industrial dispute" in s.2(q), which has the same three heads.

4. Recognition: negotiating union and council (Code s.14)

IN FORCE The Code requires a negotiating union or a negotiating council in an industrial establishment that has a registered Trade Union (s.14(1)).

One registered union only: the employer recognises it as sole negotiating union, subject to prescribed criteria (s.14(2)).
More than one registered union: the share of workers on the muster roll, verified as prescribed, decides.
51% or more: sole negotiating union (s.14(3))
No union at 51%: negotiating council (s.14(4))
Seat only for unions with 20% or more
  • The council has representatives of unions that have the support of not less than 20% of the workers on the muster roll. It has one representative for each 20%, and for the remainder after calculating the membership on each 20%.
  • Trap: the 51% test is for establishments with more than one union. One union is recognised on prescribed criteria, not on 51%.
  • History line: the second National Commission on Labour (2002) recommended that a system of recognising the negotiating agency be placed on the statute. The IR Code s.14 now does it.

Central and State recognition (s.27). IN FORCE Where the Central Government is "of the opinion that it is necessary or expedient", it may recognise a Trade Union or federation as a Central Trade Union (CTUO), in the prescribed manner (s.27(1)). In 1946 the Government verified the membership of AITUC and IFL (past event, not a Code rule).

  • EPFO-2017 Q55: "Which is not a trade union security measure?" Key (c) open shop. Sources differ: one US textbook lists the open shop as the weakest form of union security, and defines it as employers refusing to recognise any union. Learn (c) for this exam.

Problems of Indian unions

  • Multiplicity of unions is the rule in India, and rivalry causes violent incidents (VVGNLI).
  • The second National Commission on Labour (2002) says the movement is marked by multiplicity of unions, fragmentation and politicisation.
  • Outside leadership has a historical background and lingered after independence (one source, VVGNLI). Illiteracy, backwardness and fatalism of workers contributed to dependence on it (one source, VVGNLI).
  • Weak coverage: the same Commission says working-class organisations are weakened by fragmentation, disenchantment and poor unionisation.
  • Data point (Labour Bureau, 2018): average membership per registered union was 2,537, and only 4,771 unions (7.2%) filed returns.

6. Thinkers and types (retrieval cards)

Hoxie: how many functional types? Which one is NOT his? (EPFO-2017 Q49)

Exam answer: five types: business, friendly or uplift, revolutionary, predatory and dependent unionism. Evolutionary unionism is not one of them. Key (d).

Qualifier: Hoxie's own 1914 essay says "four" types (business, uplift, revolutionary, predatory) and mentions dependent unionism as a suggested further type. The 1917 book (Commons's introduction) says five.

Marx on trade unions

Unions should be used as "a lever for the final emancipation of the working class", that is "the ultimate abolition of the wages system" (Value, Price and Profit).

Webbs: Industrial Democracy

The Webbs place trade unionism in "the industrial democracy of the future". The earliest use of "collective bargaining" the Webbs knew was by Beatrice Potter in 1891 (their own footnote).

Perlman: A Theory of the Labor Movement

American unionism became a stabilised movement when scarcity consciousness replaced abundance consciousness (Perlman). Perlman says American conservative union leaders have striven more relentlessly for full job control than their radical rivals.

8. Right to strike

Exam answer (APFC-2023 Q39): "The right of workers to strike is a common law right." Key (c). Sources differ: no Indian judgment read calls it a common law right.

What the sources say

  • Art 19(1)(c) gives citizens the right "to form associations or unions or co-operative societies" (text from a mirror of the Constitution; the official copy was not reached).
  • All India Bank Employees' Association v National Industrial Tribunal (judgment 28 August 1961, AIR 1962 SC 171): the right to form unions under Art 19(1)(c) does not carry a right to strike.
  • The same case: the right to strike or to lock-out may be controlled or restricted by appropriate industrial legislation.
  • T.K. Rangarajan v Government of Tamil Nadu (6 August 2003): employees have no fundamental right to strike. There is no fundamental, legal or equitable right to go on strike. The Court also said there is no legal or statutory right to strike. That case was about government employees only.

IR Code (IN FORCE): the Code regulates strikes (ss.2(zk), 62, 63)

  • Strike (s.2(zk)): a cessation of work by a body of persons employed in any industry acting in combination, or a concerted refusal to work. It includes concerted casual leave on a given day by 50% or more of the workers.
  • No person in an industrial establishment may strike, in breach of contract, without notice within 60 days before striking. Other bars in s.62(1): within 14 days of the notice, before the date in the notice, during conciliation and for 7 days after, during Tribunal proceedings and for 60 days after, during notified arbitration and for 60 days after, and while a settlement or award applies.
  • A strike started in breach of s.62 is illegal (s.63(1)(i)).

The Code regulates strikes (ss.62, 63), but the key stays as the exam answer.
