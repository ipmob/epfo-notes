03 Collective bargaining, grievance and discipline

IN FORCE The Industrial Relations Code 2020 (IR Code) is in force from 21 November 2025 (S.O. 5320(E)). This note teaches the Code first. The old Acts appear only as a short bridge.

REPEALED The Trade Unions Act 1926, the Industrial Employment (Standing Orders) Act 1946 (IESO Act) and the Industrial Disputes Act 1947 (ID Act) stand repealed: IR Code s.104(1), as substituted by the IR Code (Amendment) Act 2026 (Act 1 of 2026). That Amendment Act is deemed in force from 21 November 2025 (its s.1(2)). The repeal date comes from reading the s.1(3) notification date with s.104(1). No single sentence of the Code states it.

IN FORCE The IR (Central) Rules 2026 (G.S.R. 342(E), 8 May 2026) are in force on publication. They govern Central-sphere establishments only. State rules may differ, and this note has not checked them.

IN FORCE The Model Standing Orders 2026 (S.O. 2312(E), 8 May 2026) say they come into force on publication. The Schedule headings still print the word "Draft".

VOLUNTARY, NOT LAW The Code of Discipline calls itself an "essentially moral agreement". It has internal sanctions in its own machinery, not legal penalties. A search of the IR Code and the Central Rules 2026 finds no mention of it. The Inter-Union Code of Conduct and the Industrial Truce Resolution are taught here as history. This note claims no in-force status for them.

Snapshot (keep these five)

  1. Bargaining route today: IR Code s.14. More than one union: the union with 51% of the muster roll is the sole negotiating union (s.14(3)). If none has 51%, a negotiating council forms from unions with 20% or more (s.14(4)). Recognition lasts 3 years, up to 5 in all (s.14(6)). IN FORCE
  2. Grievance Redressal Committee (GRC), IR Code s.4: 20 or more workers, equal members, cap ten (old Act: six), chair rotates every year. Next step: 60 days to the conciliation officer, through the union (s.4(8)). IN FORCE
  3. Model Grievance Procedure (1958): step 1 48 hours, step 2 3 days, step 3 Committee 7 days, step 4 management 3 days (not 7). Voluntary arbitration is a joint reference by union and management within a week.
  4. Discipline, IR Code s.38: suspended worker's inquiry ordinarily within 90 days. Subsistence allowance 50% for the first 90 days, 75% after, if the delay is not the worker's doing. The rates are old. The new element is the 90-day limit. IN FORCE
  5. Exam keys (kept for their year): Giri, key (d) Arbitration (PYQ-EPFO-2015-Q60), EXAM-CONVENTION. GRC, key (c) chair nominated by Government is incorrect (PYQ-EOAO-2023-Q20). Subsistence Allowance matched with the Standing Orders Act 1946 (PYQ-EOAO-2023-Q13).

1. Collective bargaining

Meaning. ILO Convention No. 154, Article 2, treats collective bargaining as all negotiations between an employer, a group of employers or employers' organisations on one side, and one or more workers' organisations on the other. Their purposes are to determine working conditions and terms of employment, to regulate relations between employers and workers, and to regulate relations between employers (or their organisations) and a workers' organisation.

Thinker. Beatrice Webb used the term "collective bargaining" in her 1891 book The Co-operative Movement in Great Britain. Write "used". Her first use is not proven, so "coined" claims too much.

Features commonly listed. Bipartite, continuous, flexible, a group action, and a process of give and take. Its outcome is a collective agreement. This list comes from a university course text. It is not a statutory list.

In concessionary bargaining the unions give concessions in a crisis.

2. Giri approach and the voluntary codes

Giri approach. V.V. Giri was President of India from 24 August 1969 to 24 August 1974. A 1952 commentary in the Economic Weekly called him "a firm believer in negotiation and voluntary conciliation" who "hates compulsory arbitration". Courts and tribunals were a last resort. The label "Giri approach" is the commentator's, and Giri disliked it. Giri's own book and the National Commission on Labour (1969) report were not reached.

Dated steps.

  1. Oct 1957 The tripartite Standing Labour Committee (16th session) adopts the Code of Discipline, with certain modifications.
  2. May 1958 16th Indian Labour Conference, Nainital, 19 and 20 May 1958. The Code of Discipline gets its 1958 form here. The Inter-Union Code of Conduct is among the papers of this session. The exact adoption date is not stated, so learn "1958". The Model Grievance Procedure was evolved at the same session.
  3. Nov 1962 Industrial Truce Resolution, adopted by a joint meeting of the central organisations of employers and workers, during an emergency. This rests on a single academic source.
  4. 21 Nov 2025 IR Code s.14 in force: the statutory route to a negotiating union. IN FORCE

Text version: Standing Labour Committee October 1957; 16th ILC Nainital May 1958; Truce Resolution November 1962; IR Code s.14 from 21 November 2025.

Correction for the coaching notes. The 16th ILC was held at Nainital on 19 and 20 May 1958, not in 1957. The 15th ILC was at New Delhi on 11 and 12 July 1957.

The Code of Discipline says. Three verified points.

  • No unilateral action by either side on any industrial matter.
  • Use the grievance procedure.
  • Recognise unions.

The text is from a reproduced booklet, not the Gazette. It sets recognition criteria of 15% of the workers in the establishment (plant level) and 25% of the workers of the industry in the local area (industry level). The criteria are from a reproduced annex of the Code, not a Gazette copy.

3. Grievance

Definition (as cited). A grievance is any discontent or dissatisfaction, whether expressed or not, whether valid or not, arising out of something connected with the company that an employee thinks, believes or feels is unfair, unjust or inequitable. This is Jucius's definition as cited in two Indian personnel texts. The original book was not opened. The IR Code does not define "grievance". The handling models are the open-door type and the step-ladder type.

Model Grievance Procedure. 1958 MODEL PROCEDURE It was evolved at the 16th ILC in May 1958. The periods below come from a 1987 ILO reproduction of the Procedure headed "India, 1958". The Ministry original was not reached.

  1. Aggrieved worker to the officer designated by management. The answer is due within 48 hours.
  2. Worker to the head of department. The answer is due within 3 days.
  3. Worker to the Grievance Committee. The Committee makes its recommendations to the manager within 7 days of the worker's request.
  4. Management gives its final decision. The period is 3 days from receiving the Committee's recommendations.
  5. Voluntary arbitration. The union and the management jointly may refer the grievance within a week of the worker receiving management's decision. It is not the worker's own right.

Text version: 48 hours, then 3 days, then 7 days, then 3 days, then arbitration within a week.

Trap: the Committee has 7 days and management has 3. Coaching notes give 7 days for management, which is wrong for this step. They also say step 1 goes to a "departmental representative". The Procedure says "the officer designated by management".

Grievance Redressal Committee under the Code. IN FORCE IR Code s.4 sits in Chapter II "Bi-partite forums", next to the Works Committee (s.3).

Question Rule Section
Who must have one? Every industrial establishment with 20 or more workers. It is a duty, unlike the Works Committee, which exists only by Government order. s.4(1)
Members? An equal number of employer and worker members. The total shall not exceed ten. s.4(2), s.4(4)
Women? Women's representation must be at least in proportion to the share of women workers. s.4(4) proviso
Chair? Chosen alternately from employer's and workers' representatives, on rotation every year. s.4(3)
Filing? Within 1 year from the date the cause of action arises. s.4(5)
Time to decide? The Committee may complete within 30 days of receipt. The word is "may", not "shall". s.4(6)
Decision? By majority view, but only if more than half of the worker members agree. Otherwise it is deemed that no decision could be reached. s.4(7)
Next step? Within 60 days the worker applies to the conciliation officer, through the Trade Union of which the worker is a member. s.4(8)

The manner of choosing members, filing and applying depends on rules. The Central Rules 2026 cover Central-sphere establishments only.

Bridge. REPEALED ID Act s.9C required a Committee in establishments with 20 or more workmen, with equal members, a rotating chair and a cap of six members. If there were two members, one woman member "as far as practicable". An aggrieved workman could appeal to the employer, who had one month to dispose of it. The Code drops the employer appeal and moves an unresolved grievance to conciliation. The cap rose from six to ten. IN FORCE IR Code s.4.

The ID Act chapter on grievance committees (Chapter IIB) was first inserted by Act 46 of 1982 and substituted by Act 24 of 2010, s.6, w.e.f. 15 September 2010. Coaching notes say "inserted 2010", which is loose.

4. Discipline

Textbook labels, not words of the Code.

  • Positive discipline: training of the mind towards self-control.
  • Negative discipline: punitive, corrective or autocratic discipline with penalties.
  • Progressive discipline escalates: oral reprimand, then written reprimand, then second written warning, then temporary suspension, then dismissal or discharge.

These definitions come from a university course text (IGNOU).

Misconduct: examples from the Model Standing Orders 2026. IN FORCE The list is in Schedule A (mining) cl.24(5), Schedule B (manufacturing) cl.23(5) and Schedule C (service) cl.24(5), in the same words for the shared items. Schedule A has 23 items, (a) to (w). The service schedule has 24, with (x) added.

Item Misconduct
(a) Theft, fraud or dishonesty in connection with the employer's business or property
(c) Wilful insubordination or disobedience, alone or with others, of any lawful or reasonable order of a superior. The order "shall normally be in writing"
(d) Habitual late attendance and habitual absence without leave or without sufficient cause
(e) Drunkenness, fighting, or riotous, disorderly or indecent behaviour while on duty at the place of work
(r) Going on an illegal strike, singly or with others, without fourteen days' previous notice

"Habitual" means committing the misconduct three or more times in the preceding twelve months. The list also covers bribes, habitual neglect of work, wilful damage to property, sleeping on duty, malingering or slowing down, conviction for an offence involving moral turpitude, continuous absence of more than ten days without permission, false information at appointment, threatening or assaulting a superior or co-worker, disclosing confidential information, refusing a charge-sheet, and sexual harassment under the 2013 Act.

Procedure under the Model Standing Orders 2026. IN FORCE Clause numbers are the Schedule A (mining) numbers, cl.24. The procedure clauses in the other schedules were not checked.

  1. Employer suspends. The worker may be suspended pending investigation or enquiry into complaints or charges of misconduct (cl.24(1)). Subsistence allowance is subject to the condition that the worker takes no employment elsewhere during the suspension (cl.24(3)).
  2. Statement of reasons. The employer gives the worker a statement setting out in detail the reasons for suspension within a week of the suspension (cl.24(6)(b)).
  3. Enquiry. The worker may appear in person, or be represented by an office-bearer of a Trade Union of which he is a member, or by a co-worker of his choice (cl.24(6)(c)). The text does not mention lawyers, so do not say they are barred. The proceedings are recorded in Hindi, English or the language of the State, whichever the charged worker prefers (cl.24(6)(d)).
  4. Time. The enquiry proceedings are completed within 90 days. The enquiry officer may extend the period for reasons recorded in writing (cl.24(6)(e)).
  5. Finding and penalty. If the worker is found guilty, he gets a reasonable opportunity to make a representation on the proposed penalty. The employer then passes an order of dismissal, suspension, fine, stoppage of annual increment or reduction in rank, if it considers that penalty meets the ends of justice (cl.24(6)(f)). The authority weighs the gravity of the misconduct, the worker's antecedents and any extenuating or aggravating circumstances. A copy of the order goes to the worker (cl.24(7)).
  6. Effect on pay. On dismissal, the worker is treated as absent for the suspension and gets no remuneration for it. The subsistence allowance already paid is not recovered. On fine, stoppage of increment or reduction in rank, the worker is treated as on duty and gets wages less the subsistence allowance paid. If not guilty, the worker is deemed on duty for the suspension and gets the same wages as if not suspended, less the subsistence allowance paid (cl.24(6)(g)).
  7. Appeal. The worker may appeal within 21 days of receiving the order to the appellate authority the employer specifies. That authority hears the worker and passes an order within 15 days of receiving the appeal (cl.24(8)).

Text version: suspend; reasons within a week; enquiry with representation; 90 days; finding and penalty; pay effect; appeal in 21 days, decided in 15.

Two different 90-day clocks. The enquiry officer's limit is 90 days, extendable for recorded reasons (cl.24(6)(e)). Separately, cl.24(2) asks for investigation and enquiry together, ordinarily within 90 days of suspension. Do not merge them. The Model Standing Orders list five penalties and do not label them minor or major. Those labels come from Government service rules.

Natural justice.

  • Two limbs: audi alteram partem (hear the other side) and nemo judex in causa sua (no one is a judge in his own cause).
  • A.K. Kraipak v. Union of India, decided 29 April 1969, AIR 1970 SC 150.
  • A reasoned order is also expected: Kranti Associates v. Masood Ahmed Khan (2010) 9 SCC 496, para 51.

Supreme Court stages of a domestic enquiry. Kushal Bhan (1960) and Firestone Tyre (1973, 1981) give these stages: a charge-sheet first; notice and a chance to defend; an enquiry officer who is not a witness; findings; punishment by the employer.

Subsistence allowance and the s.38 clock. IN FORCE IR Code s.38.

Point Rule Section
Time limit Where a worker is suspended, the investigation or inquiry (or both, if an investigation is followed by an inquiry) shall be completed ordinarily within 90 days from the date of suspension. "Ordinarily" makes it a norm, not an absolute bar. s.38(1)
Duty The standing orders "shall provide" that the employer pays subsistence allowance during suspension. s.38(2)
First 90 days 50% of the wages the worker was entitled to immediately before suspension. s.38(3)(a)
Remaining period 75% of those wages, if the delay in completing the proceedings is not directly attributable to the worker's conduct. s.38(3)(b)

Bridge. REPEALED IESO Act s.10A(1) already gave 50% for the first ninety days and 75% for the remaining period, on the same condition. The Act had no time limit for completing the inquiry. The Code's new element is the 90-day limit. The Code routes the duty through the standing orders. The IESO Act put it on the employer directly. IN FORCE IR Code s.38.

7. Check yourself

Q1 (pattern: definition-recall; seed PYQ-EPFO-2015-Q60, adapted). A 1952 commentary described the "Giri approach" as favouring which route to settling disputes, and as hostile to what?

Answer: It favoured negotiation and voluntary conciliation, and it was hostile to compulsory arbitration. The label is the commentator's. The publisher's key for Q60 is (d) Arbitration, EXAM-CONVENTION. Basis:

Q2 (pattern: old-act-applicability; seed PYQ-EPFO-2015-Q60, move: old versus new). Does the Code of Discipline (1958) have legal force today? Which IR Code section sets the statutory route to a negotiating union?

Answer: No. It is a voluntary, essentially moral agreement. The statutory route is IR Code s.14: a sole negotiating union (51% where more than one union functions) or a negotiating council, valid for 3 years. IN FORCE Basis:

Q3 (pattern: which-is-not; seed PYQ-EOAO-2023-Q20, adapted to the Code). Which statement on the Grievance Redressal Committee under IR Code s.4 is NOT correct? (a) It has an equal number of employer and worker members. (b) It is required in an establishment with 20 or more workers. (c) The chair is chosen alternately from employer and worker members, every year. (d) The total members shall not be more than six.

Answer: (d). The cap is ten (s.4(4)). Six was the old ID Act s.9C figure. IN FORCE Basis:

Q4 (pattern: timeline-or-limit; seed PYQ-EOAO-2023-Q20, different move). Under IR Code s.4, the Committee has not resolved a worker's grievance. Within what period, and through whom, can the worker approach the conciliation officer?

Answer: Within 60 days, through the Trade Union of which the worker is a member (s.4(8)). The 60 days run from the decision, or from the end of the s.4(6) period with no resolution. The worker files with the Committee within 1 year of the cause of action (s.4(5)). IN FORCE Basis:

Q5 (pattern: match-the-following; seed PYQ-EOAO-2023-Q13, adapted). In 2023 the key matched "Subsistence Allowance" with the Industrial Employment (Standing Orders) Act 1946. Which provision would the matching name today, and is it in force?

Answer: IR Code 2020, s.38. It is in force from 21 November 2025. The IESO Act is repealed, so the 2023 pairing would change. Basis:

Q6 (pattern: threshold-recall; seed PYQ-EOAO-2023-Q13, different move). What are the subsistence allowance rates in IR Code s.38(3), and when does the higher rate apply?

Answer: 50% of wages for the first 90 days of suspension. 75% for the remaining period, if the delay in completing the proceedings is not directly attributable to the worker. The rates were already in IESO Act s.10A(1), so the Code's new element is the ordinary 90-day limit on the inquiry (s.38(1)). IN FORCE Basis:

8. Also know

  • Rule 9(1) of the Central Rules lists the matters for negotiation. They include classification of grades and categories, orders under the standing orders, wages (wage period, dearness allowance, bonus, increment and allowances), hours of work and shifts, leave and holidays, promotion and transfer policy and disciplinary procedures, quarter allotment, safety, health and working conditions, other conditions of service, and any other matter agreed. Central sphere only.
  • Works Committees and GRCs are called "preventive" forums in commentary. That is a textbook label, not a statutory term. The Code heading is "Bi-partite forums".
  • The Works Committee's duty is to promote amity and good relations between employer and workers, comment on matters of common interest, and try to compose material differences (s.3(3)). IN FORCE
  • A Works Committee exists where 100 or more workers are employed, or were on any day in the preceding 12 months, and the appropriate Government orders one (s.3(1)). IN FORCE
  • Where does a "within a week" come from in the Model Grievance Procedure? From a revision petition and from the joint reference to arbitration. Neither is the management's decision period.
  • Trap for the Giri key: a coaching note gives (b) "compulsory collective bargaining". No official UPSC key could be opened, so keep (d) for this paper.
