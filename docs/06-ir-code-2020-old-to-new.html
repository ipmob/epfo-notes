06 Industrial Relations Code 2020: old Acts to the Code

IN FORCE The Industrial Relations Code 2020 (IR Code) is in force from 21 November 2025 (S.O. 5320(E)). Section 1(3) lets the Central Government fix different dates for different provisions. S.O. 5320(E) fixes 21 November 2025 for "the provisions of the said Code" and names no exception, so this note treats every IR Code section as in force. That reading rests on the one sentence of the notification in our sources. The full notification text is not saved.

REPEALED The Trade Unions Act 1926, the Industrial Employment (Standing Orders) Act 1946 (IESO Act) and the Industrial Disputes Act 1947 (ID Act) stand repealed: IR Code s.104(1), as substituted by the IR Code (Amendment) Act 2026 (Act 1 of 2026). That Amendment Act is deemed in force from 21 November 2025 (its s.1(2)). The repeal date comes from reading the s.1(3) notification date with s.104(1). No single sentence of the Code states it. IN FORCE from 21 Nov 2025

Old bodies: under s.104(1A), inserted by Act 1 of 2026, the Tribunals and authorities working under the repealed Acts continue until the matching bodies become functional under the Code. IN FORCE This note does not say whether any Code Tribunal has been notified.

IN FORCE Central Rules: the Industrial Relations (Central) Rules 2026 were notified on 8 May 2026 as G.S.R. 342(E). Rule 1(2) brings them into force on the date of publication in the Gazette. The Rules were notified 8 May 2026, almost six months after the Code began. The saved text gives only the notification date, so this note treats 8 May 2026 as the publication date. The Rules apply to the establishments they cover. State rules may differ.

IN FORCE Model Standing Orders 2026: S.O. 2312(E), 8 May 2026, made under IR Code s.29(1). The order is in force from publication. The Schedules still print the heading "Draft Model Standing Orders", but the order itself is a final order.

Rests on rules: a rule marked "rests on rules" works through rules or notifications that this note does not cover. Old-Act sections below are a bridge for old-era PYQs. Where the two differ, this note teaches the Code.

The IR Code gives the category a statutory home. Two Codes define it, and each carries part of the gratuity rule.

Point Rule Status

Expiry is not retrenchment. Section 2(zh)(iv) leaves out of "retrenchment" the termination of service on completion of the tenure of fixed term employment. Section 70 (one month's notice or pay, compensation of 15 days' average pay per completed year, notice to Government) applies to a worker who is "retrenched" after one year or more of continuous service. On a reading of the two texts, s.70 does not apply when the term ends by completion. Early termination is not covered by clause (iv). The Code has no sentence saying "no compensation".

Bridge. Section 2(zh)(iii) also leaves out non-renewal of any contract on expiry, or termination under a stipulation in it. ID Act s.2(oo)(bb), inserted in 1984, already did this. REPEALED The old exclusion is not new. The Code adds the named category with parity and gratuity provisos. The ID Act does not use the term "fixed term".

History. A Ministry of Labour and Employment notification of 7 October 2016 first allowed fixed-term employment in apparel manufacturing only. The notification of 16 March 2018 (G.S.R. 235(E)) removed the words "in apparel manufacturing sector", so the category covered all sectors. PIB releases give the dates of both notifications (7 October 2016; G.S.R. 235(E), 16 March 2018). We read the Gazette text of the 2018 step through a third-party copy. Old law, bridge only.

Q1 (seed PYQ-EPFO-2025-Q6, adapted to the Code). Which case gave the triple test, and in which IR Code section is "industry" now defined?

Q2 (seed PYQ-EPFO-2025-Q8, adapted to the Code). Under IR Code s.62(1)(a), who must give a strike notice, and within what window?

Answer: any person employed in an industrial establishment must give the employer notice within 60 days before striking (s.62(1)(a)). The duty is not limited to public utility services. The old ID Act s.22 had a six-week window for public utility services only. IN FORCE Basis:

Q3 (seed PYQ-EOAO-2023-Q13, adapted to the Code). Which IR Code section holds subsistence allowance, and what are the rates?

Answer: s.38 (Chapter IV, standing orders). Rate: 50% of the wages immediately before the suspension, for the first 90 days of suspension (s.38(3)(a)), then 75% for the remaining period if the delay is not directly attributable to the worker (s.38(3)(b)). The investigation or inquiry (or both) is ordinarily to be completed within 90 days (s.38(1)). IN FORCE Basis:

Q4 (seed PYQ-EOAO-2023-Q15, adapted to the Code). After the appellate authority's order on standing orders, in how many days do the orders come into operation under s.33(1)?

Answer: 7 days from the date copies of the appellate authority's order are sent. With no appeal, 30 days from the date authenticated copies are sent, for orders certified under s.30(8). For an adopted model order, the start date is set by Rule 10(6), not by this answer. This answer does not cover orders deemed certified after the 60-day lapse. The appeal itself must be filed within 60 days of receipt of the order (s.32). IN FORCE Basis:

Q5 (seed PYQ-EOAO-2023-Q17, adapted to the Code). Does the IR Code exempt establishments of any State from the interim model standing orders?

Answer: No exemption in s.29(2) itself. Section 39 lets the appropriate Government exempt establishments by notification. Section 29(2) deems the Central Government's model standing orders adopted from the date s.29 becomes applicable to the establishment until the certified orders operate. It has no Gujarat or Maharashtra carve-out. The saved Code text has no State exception; State standing-orders laws are outside our sources. The old IESO s.12A(2) named those two States and is repealed. REPEALED IN FORCE Basis:

Q6 (seed PYQ-EPFO-2017-Q51, adapted to the Code). Under IR Code s.37, where does a question on the application or interpretation of a certified standing order go?

Answer: to the Tribunal (the Industrial Tribunal under s.44), within whose territorial limits the establishment lies. Its decision is final and binding. A two-member bench decides it (s.44(7)(a)).
